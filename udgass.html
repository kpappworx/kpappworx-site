The idea, drawn with made-up numbers

The problem

Most businesses forget what they already learned.

Years of sales, orders and stock levels sit in spreadsheets and in different tools. When it is time to decide how much to order, someone has to piece it together by hand. What they decided last time usually isn’t written down anywhere, so every decision starts from zero.

Demand moves. Seasons overlap. A promotion makes one week look strange. A supplier runs late and everything after it shifts. It is hard to tell what is normal.

Udgass is being built to keep that memory, point out what changed, say what might happen next, and check afterwards whether it was right. We are starting with how much stock to buy.

How it is meant to work

It learns from what happened, and checks itself.

One loop, eight steps: Connect → Remember → Detect → Diagnose → Predict → Recommend → Verify → Learn. Picture a careful colleague with a perfect memory. It reads your records, notices changes, works out likely reasons, says what may happen, and suggests what to do. Then it checks how it did.

    How Udgass shows its work

    A few simple symbols, used the same way everywhere.

    Every screen Udgass will show uses the same few symbols, so you can tell at a glance what was recorded, what is an estimate, and what nobody knows yet. This is how we plan to design it. It isn’t a screenshot.

    • Evidence

      A solid dot: something that was really recorded. You should always be able to trace it back to where it came from.

    • Memory

      Layers of lines, one for each year or period. Older ones stay visible underneath.

    • Relationship

      A dotted line between two things that move together. Dotted means “seems connected”, never “caused”.

    • Something unusual

      Something breaks the usual pattern. This is the only place we use orange.

    • Prediction

      A line turns dashed where the records stop and a guess begins.

    • Uncertainty

      A shape that widens the further ahead we look. We never trim it to look tidier.

    • Decision

      A fork: the options you looked at, and the one a named person chose.

    • Outcome

      A closed circle: what really happened, shown next to what was predicted.

    How we’ll build it

    Six promises about how it will work.

    These are our design rules. They describe the plan, not a finished product.

    It only reads your data

    Udgass reads your business data. It never changes it, and it doesn’t act inside the systems it learns from.

    You make the decision

    Udgass advises and you choose. We plan for it to record what you chose and why.

    Reasons and limits, always

    Every forecast and suggestion should show what it is based on, what it assumed, and a range instead of false precision.

    Knowing versus guessing

    What was recorded, what is a likely explanation, what is only a guess, and what is missing are all labelled separately.

    No made-up cause and effect

    Udgass shouldn’t say your action caused a result unless the evidence supports it. When it can’t tell, it says so.

    Checked against reality

    We track whether forecasts and suggestions were right, and we will publish results only when they are real.

    Questions

    What people ask first.

    Can I use Udgass today?

    Not yet. Udgass is still being built, and there is nothing to sign in to. The charts on this page use made-up numbers to show the idea. The early-access list is how we will keep you posted.

    What is Udgass, in plain words?

    Think of it as a memory and a second opinion for your business. It reads your past records, notices when something changes, works out likely reasons, says what is likely to happen next, and suggests what to do. Later it checks whether it was right. We are starting with how much stock to buy.

    Will it make decisions or act for us?

    No. It only gives advice, and it only reads your data. It shows its reasons and how unsure it is, and you decide.

    What would it need from me?

    To start, your past sales and stock numbers, for example a spreadsheet export. Joining the list doesn’t mean sharing any data.

    Is it only for small shops, or only for forecasting?

    No to both. We start with stock planning because those choices repeat and you can see whether they worked. Udgass is meant for planners, supply-chain teams and owners, and for single departments in bigger companies that want to try it on one problem.

    How will we know it works?

    We will compare what it predicted with what happened, and with simple methods like a spreadsheet average. We will share results only when we have real ones.
