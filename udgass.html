Introducing Udgass by KPAppWorx

Your business has seen the pattern before.

Udgass learns from historical demand patterns to help businesses anticipate what comes next. Turn years of sales and operational data into clearer forecasts and smarter decisions.

Remember the past. Anticipate what’s next.

The problem

Your data remembers. Your forecasts should learn from it.

Businesses accumulate years of sales, pricing, inventory, and operational data. But extracting useful patterns from that history and translating them into reliable decisions remains difficult.

Demand changes. Products behave differently. Seasonal effects overlap. Promotions distort normal patterns. Supplier delays create downstream problems.

Udgass is being built to help businesses understand those patterns and anticipate demand more intelligently.

How Udgass is designed to work

From history, to pattern, to what comes next.

Three stages, one idea: a business’s past is the best evidence it has about its future.

  1. STAGE 1

    Remember

    Udgass analyses historical business data, including sales, inventory, prices, and other relevant signals.

    • Sales
    • Inventory
    • Prices
    • Other signals
  2. STAGE 2

    Recognise

    The engine identifies recurring patterns, relevant historical situations, and relationships that may help explain changes in demand.

    • Recurring patterns
    • Similar situations
    • Relationships
  3. STAGE 3

    Anticipate

    Forecasting models combine those signals to estimate future demand and help businesses plan more effectively.

    • Demand estimate
    • Range of uncertainty

The intended approach, stated plainly. Finding similar moments in a business’s history is one signal among several, not a guarantee of a better forecast. Udgass is meant to combine such signals with forecasting models and to judge every forecast against what actually happened. We will publish results only once there are real ones to show.

    Design principles

    How we intend to build it.

    These are the principles guiding the work, not claims about a finished product.

    Evidence over guesswork

    Forecasts should be evaluated against actual outcomes.

    Uncertainty made visible

    Predictions should communicate uncertainty rather than imply perfect foresight.

    Business context matters

    Historical patterns should be interpreted alongside relevant conditions.

    Decisions over dashboards

    The goal is to help businesses make better decisions, not simply display more charts.
