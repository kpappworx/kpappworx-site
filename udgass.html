The problem

Your data remembers. Your decisions should learn from it.

Businesses accumulate years of sales, pricing, inventory, and operational data, often spread across several systems. Connecting it, noticing what has changed, working out why, and turning that into a decision is largely manual. What was decided, and what happened next, is often not recorded, so the next decision starts from scratch.

Demand changes. Products behave differently. Seasonal effects overlap. Promotions distort normal patterns. Supplier delays create downstream problems.

Udgass is being built to remember, explain, predict, recommend, and learn, beginning with demand and inventory decisions.

How Udgass is designed to work

From connected data, to a decision, to a measured result.

One loop, eight steps: Connect → Remember → Detect → Diagnose → Predict → Recommend → Verify → Learn. A business’s past is the best evidence it has about its future, and what happens after each decision is the best evidence about that decision.

  3. STEP 3Direction

    Detect

    It looks for meaningful departures from the usual pattern, such as unusual demand or slower suppliers, and tries to tell a real change from ordinary fluctuation.

    • Meaningful change
    • Not noise
  4. STEP 4Direction

    Diagnose

    When something changes, it investigates possible causes by testing them against the evidence, and keeps observed facts apart from hypotheses and missing evidence.

    • Facts
    • Hypotheses
    • Missing evidence
  5. STEP 5First focus

    Predict

    It forecasts what may happen next as a range, not a single number, and compares scenarios using lead times, costs, and constraints.

    • Range, not a point
    • Scenarios
  6. STEP 6Direction

    Recommend

    It proposes actions together with the evidence, the assumptions, and the trade-offs. People review and decide. Udgass does not act on its own.

    • Evidence
    • Assumptions
    • People decide
  7. STEP 7First focus

    Verify

    It records what it predicted and recommended, then compares that with what actually happened. It measures forecast accuracy and recommendation outcomes, and says so when there is not enough evidence to claim a cause.

    • Forecast accuracy
    • Outcomes
    • No unearned causal claims
  8. STEP 8First focus

    Learn

    It uses those measured results to improve how it works for that business over time, and shows how its accuracy changes.

    • Calibration
    • Improves with results

    How Udgass shows its work

    One small visual language for evidence.

    Every screen Udgass is designed to produce uses the same marks, so a reader can tell at a glance what is known, what is estimated and what is unknown. These are design principles for the product, not a screenshot of it.

    • Evidence

      A solid point: something that was actually recorded. It always links back to its source.

    • Memory

      Layered lines, one per period. Older layers sit lower and stay visible.

    • Relationship

      A dotted link: two things that move together. Dotted means “related”, never “caused”.

    • Exception

      A break in the usual pattern. The only place the warm ember colour is used.

    • Prediction

      Solid becomes dashed where the record ends and an estimate begins.

    • Uncertainty

      A fan that widens with distance. The range is never trimmed to look neater.

    • Decision

      A fork: the options considered, and the one a named person chose.

    • Outcome

      A closed circle: what actually happened, set beside what was predicted.

    Design principles

    How we intend to build it.

    These are the principles guiding the work, not claims about a finished product.

    Read-only data access

    Udgass is designed to read business data, never to change it. It would not write to or act inside the systems it learns from.

    People keep the decision

    Recommendations are advisory. People decide, and Udgass is designed to record what they chose and why.

    Evidence, assumptions, uncertainty

    Every forecast and recommendation should come with the evidence behind it, the assumptions it makes, and a range instead of false precision.

    Facts kept apart from hypotheses

    Observed facts, supported explanations, unverified hypotheses, and missing evidence are labelled separately.

    No unearned causal claims

    Udgass should never say an action caused an outcome without sufficient causal evidence. Where it cannot tell, it says so.

    Measured over time

    Forecast accuracy and recommendation outcomes are tracked against what actually happened, and published only when there are real results.

    Questions

    What people ask first.

    Is Udgass available today?

    No. Udgass is in early development. There is no product to sign in to yet, and the charts on this page use synthetic data for illustration. The early-access list is how we will share progress.

    What is Udgass?

    A Business Decision Intelligence Engine: it is being built to remember a business’s history, detect meaningful changes, investigate likely causes, predict outcomes, recommend actions, and learn from measured results. Demand and inventory intelligence is the first application.

    Will it make decisions or take actions for us?

    No. Udgass is designed to be advisory and to use read-only access to data. It proposes actions with their evidence, assumptions, and uncertainty. People keep the decision.

    What data would it need?

    To start, historical sales and inventory data, for example from exports or spreadsheets. Other systems are a later direction. Joining the early-access list does not require sharing any data.

    Is it only for small businesses, or only for demand forecasting?

    No on both. Demand and inventory is where we are starting because those decisions repeat and their results can be measured. Udgass is meant for planners, supply-chain teams, and business leaders, including departments in larger organisations that could run a focused pilot.

    How will we know it works?

    By comparing its forecasts with what actually happened and with simple baselines, and by tracking the outcomes of its recommendations over time. We will publish results only when there are real ones.
