The problem

Businesses record history. They don’t always learn from it.

01

What happened

Sales, orders, stock movements and supplier events sit in operational systems, each with its own timestamps and owners.

Held in: ERP, order and stock systems

02

What was decided

The reasoning behind a choice lives in meetings, messages and spreadsheets. Often only the result of the decision survives.

Held in: email, slides, personal files

03

What actually happened

Outcomes arrive later, in different reports, and are rarely tied back to the estimate and the decision that came before them.

Held in: periodic reports and reviews

Results can be measured. Which judgements held up, and why, is harder to see.

When events, decisions and outcomes stay in separate places, an organisation repeats its reasoning instead of building on it. Udgass is being designed to keep the three connected, so that each decision can be reviewed against what followed.

From business history to business intelligence.

  3. Takes in
    History and detected changes.
    Produces

    After 02 · feeds 04

  4. Takes in

    After 03 · feeds 05

  5. Takes in
    What was estimated, what was decided, and what happened.
    Produces
    A measured track record, including misses. Any change to the method is reviewed against that record rather than assumed to be an improvement.

    After 04 · returns to 01

The record from stage 05 returns to stage 01, so each cycle starts with more evidence.

Working today

Run a backtest on a file. See how it did.

This is the first working piece of Udgass. Choose a sales file and it replays your history, makes each estimate using only what was known at the time, and checks it against what happened. It runs in your browser and the file is not uploaded anywhere.

Your file

A CSV with a date column, a quantity column and, optionally, a product column. Daily or weekly rows both work. It needs at least 56 weeks of history.

Please do not use files containing personal or sensitive data. The file is read in this page only and nothing is sent to a server.

What you will see

  • Error of the estimates compared with a simple flat baseline
  • How often reality landed inside the stated range
  • Results by product, and the largest misses
  • The assumptions made about your data

The method is deliberately simple. On some data it will be no better than a flat average, and the result will say so.

Engineering principles

Reliance has to be earned.

1

Traceable evidence

Each estimate is meant to link back to the data and the date it was based on, so a reviewer can reconstruct how it was reached.

2

Explicit uncertainty

Outputs are ranges with their assumptions stated. Where the evidence is thin, the record is meant to say so.

3

Measured performance

Estimates are compared with what happened, and the results are reported, including where they were wrong.

4

Human accountability

Udgass advises and records. A named person makes and owns each decision. It is designed to read data, not to act on your systems.

Demand assessment

See how your own history would have been forecast.

You run the assessment on your own computer with your sales export. It replays past periods using only what was known at the time, compares the estimates with what happened and with simple methods, and shows where they fail. You keep the report.

  1. 01Request the assessment kit below. It runs with Python 3.9 or newer and needs nothing else.
  2. 02Export your sales history and run one command. Preparing the export is usually the longest step.
  3. 03Read the report and decide whether a pilot is worth it. The decision is yours.
What you need
A sales export with date, product and quantity, daily or weekly, ideally two years or more. Optional: stock-on-hand snapshots, purchase orders with receipt dates, and your own past forecasts for a head-to-head comparison.
What you get
A report covering data quality, which inventory questions your data can and cannot answer, accuracy against simple methods (and against your forecasts, if provided), the products where estimates fail, and an advisory view on whether a pilot is justified.
Cost
Free for the first three companies, in exchange for permission to quote the result, positive or negative, only in wording your company approves in writing. No data or confidential details are ever published. Later assessments and pilots are paid.
Your data
The assessment runs on your machine and uploads nothing. If you cannot run it yourself, data is accepted only under written terms agreed first: agreed fields only, encrypted storage, access by the founder only, deletion within 30 days with written confirmation.
