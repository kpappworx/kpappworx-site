See what your AI agents are doing, and what they cost.

Control Tower tracks every agent run for loops, token spend and slow steps, using metadata only. It never reads your prompts or outputs, and it cannot change anything in your systems.

Agents fail in ways normal monitoring cannot see.

Every call in a loop returns a healthy response. The failure only shows up later, as a bill or an incident.

Runaway loops

An agent repeats the same tool call, or two agents pass work back and forth, until a limit stops them.

  • Each call looks fine on its own
  • The cost appears at month end

Hidden token spend

Your model provider bills one number. You need it by agent, team, task and outcome.

  • Retries and failed runs still cost money
  • No link between spend and value

Behavior that drifts

An agent starts using a tool it never used, or runs at a different volume, and nobody notices.

  • No baseline of normal
  • No inventory of which agents exist

Three parts, one read-only pipeline.

A small SDK in your environment, a detection layer, and a dashboard your engineers and finance team can both use.

Lightweight SDK

Python first, built on OpenTelemetry, with LangChain and AutoGen adapters and wrappers for OpenAI and Anthropic clients.

  • Runs in the background, never blocks your agent
  • Turns off with one environment variable
  • Planned as open source so your security team can read it

Loop and anomaly detection

Rules that spot repeated calls, repeated results, cycles between agents, runaway steps, cost caps and sudden token spikes.

  • Works from hashed fingerprints, not content
  • Dry-run mode to tune before anyone is paged
  • Alerts to Slack, email or your webhook

Cost and governance dashboard

Spend, cost per successful run, latency, budgets and forecasts, with an agent inventory and an audit log.

  • Cost is labeled as an estimate from token counts
  • ROI uses the value you enter per run
  • Weekly summary email for finance

Built so there is little to approve and nothing to break.

These limits are written into the design and checked by automated tests before every release.

Metadata only
Token counts, timings, statuses, model and tool names, and hashed fingerprints. No prompts, outputs, tool arguments or end-user data.
A closed list of fields
Events with any field outside the published schema are rejected, and values that look like emails, card numbers or keys are dropped.
Write-only access
Your ingest key can send data and nothing else. We hold no credentials for your agents, tools, databases or cloud accounts.
Alerts, never actions
We tell you what happened. We do not pause, throttle or stop anything. That decision stays with you.
Fails open
If our service is slow or down, your agents are unaffected. The SDK drops data rather than waiting, and counts what it dropped.
Your data stays yours
Each customer is isolated, with its own encryption keys and a short default retention. Nothing is shared across customers.

Is Control Tower available today?

No. It is in development. The design and a 16-week build plan are complete, and we are recruiting a small number of design partners to shape the first version. We will not describe it as shipped until it is.

Does it read our prompts or model outputs?

No. The SDK sends only counts, timings, statuses, model names and hashed fingerprints of calls. Prompts, outputs, tool arguments and documents are dropped inside your process before anything leaves it, and our servers reject events that contain anything outside the published list of fields.

Can it stop or change an agent?

No, by design. Control Tower reads telemetry and sends alerts to Slack, email or a webhook you choose. It has no credentials for your systems and cannot call your agents or tools. What happens after an alert is your decision.

How is this different from Langfuse, LangSmith, Helicone or Datadog?

Those are mature products and many are good. Most capture prompts and outputs by default, which some security teams will not approve. Control Tower is metadata-only from the start, and puts loop detection and finance-grade cost views first. It is also much earlier, so if you need a proven platform today, choose one of them.

Which frameworks will it support?

The first version targets Python with an OpenTelemetry exporter, a LangChain callback handler, an AutoGen adapter and wrappers for the OpenAI and Anthropic clients. Other frameworks come later, in the order design partners ask for them.

What do design partners get?

A free pilot, direct access to the founder, and a say in what is built first. We ask for a real agent workload to test against, a regular feedback conversation by message, and honest comments on what is missing.
