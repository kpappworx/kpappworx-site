The problem

Your CRM records every change. It doesn't tell you which ones matter.

By Friday the pipeline looks different from Monday — and the edits that should change what you commit to are buried among the ones that shouldn't.

Revenue leaders

You commit to a number using a pipeline you last examined deal by deal weeks ago. The risky ones are in there somewhere.

Sales managers

Close dates move, stages slip backwards, owners change. Each edit is defensible alone. The pattern across four weeks is the story.

RevOps

Assembling the weekly review by hand means exports and pivot tables, and a report that is already stale when the meeting starts.

How it works

Connect once. Know what changed every week.

Quarterdeck reads the pipeline where it already lives. No exports, no data warehouse, nothing written back to your CRM.

01 · Connect

Authorize HubSpot, read-only

One OAuth connection. Access stays read-only and can be revoked from HubSpot without touching your data.

02 · Baseline

Build the change history

Snapshots and change events accumulate, so a one-off edit can be told apart from repeated drift.

03 · Rank

Surface material exceptions

Deterministic rules weigh value, timing, stage, staleness and repeated signals, then suppress the noise.

04 · Review

Read the brief

Open the app or the email: what changed, which deals deserve attention, and the evidence behind each one.

How it's built

Made to survive a security review, not just a demo.

The engineering standard behind every KPAppWorx product: deterministic calculations, explainable conclusions, and AI that improves language without inventing facts.

Read-only by design

Quarterdeck never writes to your CRM. No fields updated, no workflows triggered, no automation firing unexpectedly in the middle of a review.

Every conclusion carries evidence

No unexplained scores. Each flag ties to a specific missing activity, a stalled stage or a dated record already sitting in HubSpot.

A short list, not another dashboard

Five to ten deals worth reviewing this week, rather than fifty metrics to interpret before the meeting has even started.
