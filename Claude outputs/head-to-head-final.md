**16 September 2026 · Adversarial commercial test · No ranking**

Weaker than certified payroll. Medical cards renew every 12–24 months; MVRs and annual reviews are yearly. **Long stretches where the product does nothing visible** — which is the "paying for silence" problem that killed Failsafe, in a different costume.

# C. Strongest evidence for each

**Certified payroll:** the **weekly filing cadence**. Fifty forced uses a year is the best retention mechanic found in any market across all passes. Paired with a documented managed-service alternative at $1,000–5,000/month, the value gap for a small contractor is real and large.

**DOT DQF:** the **verified async purchasing motion**. 14 of 16 vendors publish self-serve prices, which proves non-technical buyers in this market complete purchases without talking to anyone. That is the single hardest thing to verify about any market and here it is established fact, not inference.

# D. Strongest disqualifying evidence for each

## Certified payroll — the category leader gives your MVP away free

Two separate findings, either of which is serious:

**1. [LCPtracker introduced a free single-project tier in November 2018](https://lcptracker.com/press-release/lcptracker-introduces-free-subscription-tier-for-single-contractor-certified-payroll-solution/).** It includes unlimited certified payroll reports for the first project, wage verification and mathematical checks, federal and state reports, payroll data import, unlimited cloud storage, California craft/classification maintenance, **XML reporting for California's DIR**, and email and phone support.

Read that feature list against the MVP wedge in §A5. It is the same product. The category leader has offered it free for seven years, and it is explicitly aimed at *"smaller subcontractors with minimal prevailing wage projects"* — the exact segment you would enter through.

**2. [The Department of Energy has contracted LCPtracker to be free to all IIJA award recipients](https://www.energy.gov/infrastructure/weekly-dba-payroll-tracking-lcptracker).** On the largest US infrastructure programme in decades, contractors and subcontractors get the mandated system at no cost, with the awarding agency paying. Where that arrangement exists, a paid alternative is competing against free-and-already-integrated.

**Plus: no switching evidence, no community, and 2 Capterra reviews in six years.** A category where the leader gives the entry product away and almost nobody reviews the paid alternatives is not a category with visible buyer choice.

## DOT DQF — the community's own answer is "don't pay for this"

In the TruckersReport thread where someone asks directly whether to hire Foley or a similar firm for paperwork and compliance ([link](https://www.thetruckersreport.com/truckingindustryforum/threads/foley-compliance-for-paperwork-help.2515085/)), the consensus response is DIY:

- Compliance companies described as *"parasites"* charging *"$150"* for tasks taking *"less than a minute"* (FloridaRetired)
- *"companies like Foley...are shady enough to spam you with email and calls so why wouldn't they be dishonest enough to abuse the suckers who actually sign up"* (RunningAces)
- A self-identified **consultant** in the thread says owners can manage the requirements *"very easily"* themselves (Brian991219)
- The summarised consensus: **DIY compliance management is consistently presented as both feasible and superior to outsourcing**

And the substitute they recommend is your MVP: *"an online calendar and set all the dates to repeat whenever something was due to keep organized"* (Iamoverit).

**This is the 85%-DIY pattern again**, in a market you reached by explicitly trying to escape it.

Compounding it: the 2.8/5 rating is about sales conduct rather than product failure, so it generates *avoidance of Foley*, not *demand for better software*. No switching evidence exists in either forum thread. Prices have converged. And the paid value sits in the consortium you'd need a C/TPA registration to provide.

## One honest qualifier

Both failures rest partly on *absence* of evidence — no switching found, no customer counts found, no community found. Absence is weaker than contradiction, and in certified payroll's case it may reflect a market that simply doesn't generate public discussion rather than one without buyers. The single question in §E — how widely agencies pay for LCPtracker — is answerable in an afternoon by reading a dozen state and municipal contract-compliance pages, and it would move certified payroll decisively in one direction or the other.

DOT DQF's failure is firmer, because it rests on positive contradicting evidence: people in the market, asked directly, told someone not to buy this.
